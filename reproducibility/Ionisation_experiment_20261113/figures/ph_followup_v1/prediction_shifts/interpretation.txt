HOW THE CATEGORICAL pH INPUTS CHANGE PREDICTIONS

These comparisons use all 327 held-out structures for every endpoint,
including structures without observed labels for that endpoint. For each
structure, subtract the prediction of the comparator from that of the first
arm at the same seed and average the nine differences. Half-life, Vd, CL,
Cmax and Tmax use log2 fold ratios. F and PPB use percentage points (pp).
A prediction ratio of 1 means the two predictions agree. Positive signed
changes mean higher predictions. The magnitudes below are absolute values
of the per-structure nine-seed averages. They describe prediction changes,
while the separate error figures use observed endpoint labels.

1. The same small centre can conceal very different compound-level changes

For shared-recipe ALL4 versus REP4, the median signed F change is about
+0.30 pp in each backbone. Median absolute F changes are 2.78 pp for
GraphConv, 4.95 pp for Chemprop and 0.96 pp for frozen ChemBERTa. In Chemprop,
170 structures rise and 157 fall, and 163 of 327 move by at least 5 pp.
Its middle 80% ranges from -10.69 to +11.06 pp. The whole-cohort signed
mean is +0.29 pp. GraphConv has 92 structures with changes of at least
5 pp, and ChemBERTa has two.

Cmax shows a similar pattern. Chemprop's median signed shift is almost zero
and the counts are 164 higher and 163 lower. The median magnitude is
0.463 log2, or 1.38-fold in either direction, and 54 structures move by at
least twofold. GraphConv and ChemBERTa median magnitudes are 0.346 and 0.109
log2 (1.27-fold and 1.08-fold). GraphConv has 25 structures that move at
least twofold, and ChemBERTa has none. Chemprop's PPB mean shift is -1.31 pp
with 138 structures rising and 189 falling. Tmax has the smallest median
magnitude of the five log endpoints in all three models (0.086, 0.111 and
0.022 log2, respectively).

2. Opposite shifts in Vd among structures with stable and changing categories

For frozen ChemBERTa, shared-recipe ALL4 versus REP4 Vd has a whole-cohort
mean shift of -0.018 log2. The 121 stable structures shift down by -0.127
log2 and the 206 switchers shift up by +0.046 log2. The mean change has
the corresponding sign in all nine paired seed trajectories for each group.
The stable group begins at a higher REP4 geometric mean predicted Vd than
the switchers, 182.0 L versus 103.3 L. With ALL4 these become 166.6 L and
106.6 L. GraphConv shows the same opposite directions, with geometric
means changing from 309.1 to 283.7 L in stable structures and 82.5 to 86.7 L
in switchers. Chemprop Vd rises more in switchers, 75.4 to 84.0 L, than in
stable structures, 290.9 to 292.8 L. These are descriptive summaries across
structures and seeds.

All 90 base-only compounds at pH 7.4 retain the same operational category
at every tested pH. For those compounds, the two 16-channel vectors are
identical, as they are for all 121 stable compounds. Even so, ChemBERTa Vd
falls in 89 of the 90 base-only compounds, with a mean log2 change of
-0.166. The geometric mean ratio is 0.891 (10.9% lower), from 238.7 to
212.7 L. For the same group, its Cmax shifts up by +0.099 log2 and F by
+1.15 pp. All three class-level mean directions hold across the nine seed
means. The models were fitted separately on their respective inputs, so
their predictions can differ even on identical vectors. The subgroup signs
are properties of these fitted models. The category names describe
threshold flags from the same two archived pKa estimates.

3. Input comparisons that change the size of prediction shifts

The individual-pH comparisons show endpoint- and backbone-dependent changes.
For example, Chemprop ADD65 versus REP2 has a median absolute F shift of
4.11 pp, with 137 structures moving by at least 5 pp despite 38 structures
changing category at pH 6.5. ChemBERTa ADD65 F moves by a median of 0.50 pp.
Across both backbones, the counts of changed input categories are fixed.

Repeating pH 7.4 alone also changes predictions. Chemprop REP4 versus
REF74 moves F by a median of 4.60 pp and at least 5 pp in 151 structures.
Adding the pH 7.4 category to structure alone (REF74 versus REF0) produces
larger median Vd shifts than ALL4 versus REP4 in all three backbones.
Those medians are 0.512, 0.547 and 0.156 log2 versus 0.266, 0.270 and
0.101 log2, in GraphConv, Chemprop and ChemBERTa respectively.

Independent tuning keeps the broad backbone difference in shift magnitude.
Tuned Chemprop Cmax moves by a median of 0.648 log2 (1.57-fold), with 88
structures moving by at least twofold, versus 54 in the shared-recipe
comparison. Its signed mean changes from -0.037 to +0.183 log2. Tuned
ChemBERTa Vd shifts remain narrower (median magnitude 0.097 log2). For
both ChemBERTa arms the tuned result shares 36 of 45 fits with the
shared-recipe result. Tuning therefore describes the sensitivity of these
predictions to recipe selection on the same data.

The figures compare outputs of separately fitted models using operational
categories. They describe shifts in the original endpoint predictions.
No endpoint was measured under the added pH conditions.

Files

structure_shifts.csv contains each compound's nine-seed mean, absolute
mean shift across seeds, seed SD and higher/lower seed counts.
shift_summary.csv contains signed and absolute summaries, interquantile
ranges and fold ratios for the entire cohort, pH 7.4 categories and
switcher/stable strata. seed_shifts.csv retains nine whole-stratum means
and magnitudes for every summary. Manifest binds the frozen prediction
export, source-fit provenance, release and categorical pH ledger.
