Figure 4. Cohort, baseline and mechanistic checks for the ionisation study. A shows all 494 supported categorical assignments (101 anionic, 163 cationic, 171 neutral, 59 zwitterionic) and the 327-structure numerical subset (65, 90, 119, 53). Colours distinguish the four audited classes. B shows held-out Vd MAE on the same 388 labelled class-cohort structures. Overall and ionisation-class medians are estimated in each training fold. Morgan ridge uses 2048-bit radius-2 fingerprints with or without the class one-hot vector; regularisation is selected in inner CV. These deterministic controls have one prediction trajectory; their lack of a seed-SD bar does not imply zero population uncertainty. Neural points are nine seeds, diamonds are means and bars are sample seed SD. No model superiority test is encoded in this absolute-error panel. C shows the C2-C0 comparison within each class, using only observed Vd labels: 79 anionic, 139 cationic, 123 neutral and 47 zwitterionic structures. The absolute paired MAE differences and 95% crossed seed/group bootstrap intervals are divided by the fixed comparator mean MAE within that model and stratum and multiplied by 100. These are exploratory intervals with no multiple-comparison adjustment. D uses all 494 structures because the triad consists of predicted endpoints and requires no measured triad. The plotted metric is mean absolute log10[t_half/(ln(2)*Vd/CL)] across structures for each seed. Faint lines connect paired seeds, with horizontal offsets solely for visibility. Diamonds and bars show the mean and sample SD of the nine trajectory values. This assesses internal algebraic agreement, not agreement with measured PK. Neither arm has a mass-balance training penalty. Frozen ChemBERTa refers to its frozen encoder with a trained MLP head.
