Figure 1. Ionisation information and Vd prediction. All points are held-out predictions pooled across five frozen parent/scaffold-group outer folds, with nine paired final-training seeds and five inner folds for model selection/calibration. The transformer is frozen and its MLP head is trained. The physiology penalty is zero; all supervised endpoint weights are one after training-only target scaling. A and C use the audited 494-structure class cohort (388 Vd labels); B and D use its 327-structure numerical-pKa subset (259 Vd labels). A compares fixed four-class one-hot input with no ionisation input. B compares eight Henderson-Hasselbalch acidic/basic propensity channels at pH 2, 5, 6.5, 7.4 with the two channels at 7.4. These are descriptor representations of archived predicted pKas, not validated organ-compartment PK simulations. In A-B, the seed values are 100 times each paired log10-MAE difference divided by the comparator MAE averaged over all nine seeds. The same fixed denominator scales the absolute-difference bootstrap interval; it is not a bootstrap of a random ratio. Thick lines show 95% and thin lines show 99.1667% equal-tailed crossed seed/group bootstrap intervals (10,000 draws), the latter accounting for six prespecified Vd comparisons. Intervals are conditional on observed data, folds and selected settings. The adjusted interval excludes zero for the Chemprop and frozen ChemBERTa category comparisons, but not GraphConv or any multi-pH comparison. C-D show absolute Vd MAE; small points are seeds, diamonds are means and bars are sample seed SD (not confidence intervals). C includes scalar -1/0/+1 input (neutral and zwitterionic both 0), a four-dimensional one-hot vector and a learned 16-dimensional category embedding. D includes no ionisation, one-hot, the raw acidic/basic pKa pair, pH-7.4 propensities, the multi-pH profile and a repeated pH-7.4 control. Both eight-channel inputs are divided by two after training-only standardisation. Shared-recipe results are shown; independent tuning sensitivities appear in Figure 3. No between-cohort performance comparison is implied.
