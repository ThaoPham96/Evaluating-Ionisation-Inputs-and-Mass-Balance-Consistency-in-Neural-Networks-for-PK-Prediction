A physiology-informed training loss improves the consistency of predicted pharmacokinetic endpoints.

(A) Training scheme. The supervised MSE loss uses available observed labels across the seven predicted endpoints. The orange mass-balance term, weighted by lambda, couples predicted half-life, volume of distribution and clearance in unstandardised log10 space. The two terms are summed before updating the network (dark dashed feedback). L_MSE denotes the supervised loss L_sup defined in Methods, with equal endpoint weights. Endpoint symbols inside the penalty match the colours of the corresponding outputs.
(B) Cumulative distribution of observed mass-balance fold disagreement among half-life, volume of distribution and clearance for 674 structures. The neutral curve shows the percentage of structures within each fold threshold; marked reference points indicate 66.9% within 1.5-fold, 80.1% within 2-fold and 95.1% within 5-fold. The logarithmic x-axis retains the full observed range.
(C) Predicted mass-balance fold disagreement without the loss (dashed grey) and with the loss (solid orange), shown separately for each architecture. Curves average nine seed-specific cumulative distributions, each based on 952 held-out structures.
(D) Change in mean absolute error over the three coupled endpoints with the loss, as a percentage of the stratum's mean structure-only error, grouped by whether the observed triad disagreement is at most 1.5-fold (451 structures) or above 1.5-fold (223 structures). Diamonds show means and bars show unadjusted 95% paired group-bootstrap intervals.
(E) Change in endpoint mean absolute error with the loss, as a percentage of mean baseline error. Dots are nine paired seeds, diamonds their means and bars show one sample SD on either side of the mean. The seven colours match the output symbols in A. Endpoint row labels are black and the equal-weight average across seven endpoints uses neutral marks.
ChemBERTA denotes frozen ChemBERTa representations with a trainable MLP head.
